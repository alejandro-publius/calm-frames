2. Stage One: pass or fail

This is the whole of it. Quoted from the rules page, section 6:

Stage One) The first stage will determine via pass/fail whether the ideas meet a baseline level of viability, in that the Project reasonably fits the theme and reasonably applies the required APIs/SDKs featured in the Hackathon.

So Stage One is two questions: does it fit the theme, and does it reasonably apply the required APIs and SDKs. Everything else that can disqualify a submission lives in the Project Requirements and Submission Requirements, which a Stage One reviewer is reading at the same time. Those are below as a checklist, each line traced to the rule it comes from.

2.1 The Fire TV track

Overview page, under "What to Build":

Fire TV Launch a demo-ready app on Fire OS or Vega OS. Use React Native, web technologies, or Android (Kotlin/Java), any framework is fine, as long as it runs on Fire OS or Vega OS.

Priority categories: AI-enhanced viewing, sports, fitness, family entertainment, multi-modal UX, and computer vision.

Rules page, Project Requirements:

Fire TV: Eligible projects for this category have to launch a demo-ready app that works on Fire OS or Vega OS. Priority categories: AI-enhanced viewing, sports, fitness, family entertainment, multi-modal UX, and computer vision.

Two of the six priority categories are ours by name: family entertainment and multi-modal UX. The rules do not say a priority category is required, and nothing on either page attaches a score to one.

2.2 The runtime technology hook, and why Fire TV is exempt

Rules page, Submission Requirements:

For Alexa+, Bee, and Ring: The repository must demonstrate use of your track's required technology at runtime in your code, imported and actually called (a library import, an app/backend entry point, or a loaded agent/flow/MCP config), not just named in the README. Fire TV is the exception, see below.

Fire TV: Any framework or language is acceptable (React Native, Android/Kotlin/Java, web technologies, etc.). The requirement is that the project runs on Fire OS or Vega OS. The demo video must show the project running on an actual Fire TV device or the Fire TV/Vega simulator.

Read this carefully. Fire TV entries are exempt from the runtime hook rule. What Fire TV entries must do instead is show the project running on an actual Fire TV device or the Fire TV/Vega simulator in the demo video. That is the hard Stage One requirement for our track, and it is a video requirement, not a code requirement.

Our README's "Required tech at runtime" table goes past what the rules ask of a Fire TV entry. That is fine and it helps Tech Implementation, but it does not replace the video.

3. Stage Two: the four criteria

Quoted from the rules page, section 6. The overview page carries the same four with slightly shorter wording.

Stage Two) All Submissions that pass Stage One will be evaluated in Stage Two based on the following equally weighted criteria (the "Judging Criteria"):

Entries will be judged on the following equally weighted criteria, and according to the sole and absolute discretion of the judges:

1. Tech Implementation

Tech Implementation: How well is the project built, and how effectively does it use the required tech? Does it effectively leverage the required APIs, SDKs, or device capabilities for the specified track (Fire TV, Alexa+, Bee, or Ring) or mini-challenge (AWS Builder, Open Source)?

2. Design

Design: Does the project deliver a complete, coherent product experience? Is the interaction model intuitive and well-considered for the target device or platform?

3. Potential Impact

Potential Impact: Does the project make a credible, specific case for solving customer needs? Could it realistically serve an audience beyond the hackathon? (Examples: Fire TV Appstore, Ring Appstore)

4. Quality of the Idea

Quality of the Idea: Is this a creative, imaginative use of the required tools? Does the team demonstrate a genuine understanding of the developer ecosystem and the end-user needs within their chosen track? Judges should distinguish creative ideas from obvious ones:

Fire TV: Obvious: basic streaming UI, simple video player, remote-controlled game. Creative: AI-enhanced viewing that adapts to household patterns, computer vision fitness app using the TV camera, multi-modal UX combining voice + D-pad

  • visual in a single flow.

AWS Builder: Obvious: single Bedrock call for text generation, S3 for storage. Creative: multi-service pipeline (Bedrock + AgentCore + Strands), agentic architecture with Claude/Kiro for development workflow, agent orchestration patterns.

Open Source: Obvious: README update, typo fix, minor formatting. Creative: meaningful feature addition with tests, bug fix that unblocks other developers, new integration pattern (e.g., adding Vega support to a popular React Native library).

Note the Fire TV example of a creative idea: "multi-modal UX combining voice + D-pad + visual in a single flow". That is the third thing our one seek function does, and the submission should say so in those words.

3.1 Tie breaking

For each Prize listed below, if two or more Submissions are tied, the tied Submission with the highest score in the first applicable criterion listed above will be considered the higher scoring Submission.

So the order matters after all. On a tie, Tech Implementation breaks first, then Design, then Potential Impact, then Quality of the Idea.

3.2 The 1 to 5 scale is ours, not Amazon's

Amazon publishes no numeric scale, no points per criterion and no rubric bands. The rules say only "equally weighted" and "the sole and absolute discretion of the judges", and mention "the submission's final spreadsheet score" without saying what is in the spreadsheet. Everything in this section is our own instrument for finding our own gaps.

For our mock judging, score each criterion 1 to 5:

Score What it means
5 Nothing a judge could reasonably ask for is missing. Claims are evidenced and the evidence is reachable in a few clicks.
4 Strong, with one named gap that does not undercut the main claim.
3 The case is made but a judge has to take something on trust that could have been shown.
2 A central claim is unevidenced, or the experience has a hole a judge will hit.
1 The criterion is not really addressed.

A score has to come with the sentence a judge would write, and a gap has to name the file or the field that would fix it.

4. The friction log bonus, worth up to 10%

Quoted from the rules page, section 6:

Bonus Points: During Stage 1 downselection, Amazon's internal review team assesses each submission's friction log entries (if provided) and passes a recommended bonus[,] up to 10%[,] to the Stage 2 judging panel along with the shortlisted projects. The Stage 2 panel applies this bonus to the submission's final spreadsheet score.

And from the Submission Requirements, the six fields every entry needs:

Optional, Friction Log Entries: specific task attempted, steps taken, expected vs. actual result, severity rating, workaround used, and actionable suggestion. Participants who submit friction logs score higher in judging (up to 10% bonus).

The overview page says the same in different words:

Optional: Friction log entries for each one: the task you attempted, the steps you took, what you expected vs. what actually happened, a severity rating, any workaround you used, and an actionable suggestion. Submissions with friction logs can earn up to a 10% judging bonus.

The six fields, to check each entry against:

  1. The specific task attempted
  2. The steps taken
  3. Expected result versus actual result
  4. A severity rating
  5. The workaround used
  6. An actionable suggestion

A mock judge should walk docs/FRICTION_LOG.md entry by entry and mark any entry that is missing one of the six. The bonus is assessed at Stage 1 by Amazon's internal review team, which means it is read by someone doing downselection at speed, so a missing field in one entry is a real cost.

Feature requests are a separate optional item with three fields:

Optional, Feature Requests: description, why it matters to their project, and priority rating (Critical / Important / Nice-to-have)

No bonus is attached to feature requests anywhere on either page.

5. The two mini challenges

Overview page:

Mini Challenges (additional opportunities alongside your primary track)

AWS Builder Use AWS services (Bedrock, AgentCore, Strands SDK, Kiro Crew, SageMaker, etc.) in your project with documented integrations, and you're in.

Building with Kiro Crew qualifies on its own, you don't also need to call a separate AWS runtime service.

Open Source Ship a new, additional open-source project (include an open-source license) or a contribution (a branch, a fork, or a pull request) to a public repository during the hackathon window, alongside your primary-track submission. PRs don't need to be merged; a forked, unmerged version is fine.

Include the contribution URL, your repo URL, your GitHub username, and a short description of what you did, how it works, and why it matters.

Rules page, Project Requirements:

AWS Builder (Mini Challenge): Any primary track project that incorporates AWS services (i.e. Amazon Bedrock, AgentCore, Strands SDK, Kiro Crew, SageMaker, etc.) with documented integrations.

Kiro Crew qualifies on its own as a development tool used during the hackathon[,] a submission does not need to also call a runtime AWS service (Bedrock, AgentCore, SageMaker, Strands SDK) to count for this mini-challenge.

Open Source (Mini Challenge): Create a new, additional open-source project or contribute to an existing public repository during the hackathon window, alongside a primary track submission. Contributions can be a new repo (with open-source license), a branch, a fork, or a pull request. PRs do not need to be merged; share your forked version if unmerged. Required: contribution URL, project repository URL, GitHub username, and a description of what you did, how it works, and why it matters.

Two things to watch on our own entry.

AWS Builder says "incorporates AWS services ... with documented integrations". The earlier undeployed-stack question was superseded by the saved October 9 deployment and successful preparation run in aws_deploy_2026-10-09.json. The integration prepares and publishes titles; the TV app still plays its bundled library and does not trust the separate cloud demo key. State that boundary and the observed results in the submission's AWS services field. This is evidence of the integration, not a guarantee of a judging outcome. Credit coverage and current budget visibility still need the billing check in the completion checklist. Kiro Crew remains unused and must not be claimed.

Open Source says "new, additional". Our reading is that the primary-track repository alone is not the mini challenge entry, and that the upstream pull request in docs/OPEN_SOURCE_PR.md is. That pull request has to exist, from a fork under a real account, inside the hackathon window, which is why STATUS.md dates it Mon Oct 12. The rules are explicit that it does not have to be merged.

Overview page, on winning:

You can layer a mini challenge onto the same project for extra ways to win, but each project can only win one track prize and one mini challenge prize.

7. Rules that shape how we build, not just how we submit

Original work, and open source inside it. Rules page, Intellectual Property:

An Entrant may submit a Submission that includes the use of open source software or hardware, provided the Entrant complies with applicable open source licenses and, as part of the Submission, creates software that enhances and builds upon the features and functionality included in the underlying open source product.

Our app/ is derived from AmazonAppDev/vega-video-sample under MIT-0, and the whole point of the entry is what we added on top of it. That is the shape the rule describes. The licence files and the credit have to stay where they are.

New and existing. Rules page, Project Requirements:

New & Existing: Projects must be either newly created by the Entrant or, if the Entrant's Project existed prior to the Hackathon Submission Period, must have been significantly updated after the start of the Hackathon Submission Period. Entrants must explain how their Project was significantly updated, clearly describe and demo the feature updates during the Submission Period.

Calm Frames was started inside the window, so this does not bind us. The derivation from the sample app is worth naming anyway, in the description as well as the licence table, so nobody has to wonder.

Functionality. Rules page, Project Requirements:

Functionality: The Project's code repository must include clear setup and run instructions, and the Project must be shown functioning in the demonstration video, as depicted in the video, URL and/or expressed in the text description.

8. What this page could not verify

Said plainly, rather than guessed at.

  1. There is no published numeric scale, no points per criterion and no score bands. The rules say "equally weighted" and refer to a "final spreadsheet score" without describing it. Our 1 to 5 scale in section 3.2 is our own.
  2. How the 10% friction log bonus is computed is not published. The rules say Amazon's internal review team "assesses each submission's friction log entries" and "passes a recommended bonus". What makes a log worth 10% rather than 3% is not stated anywhere on either page.
  3. Whether a synthesized but undeployed CDK stack counts for AWS Builder is not answerable from the page. See section 5. This one has a cost attached and should go to the hackathon manager as a question.
  4. The judges are not named. The overview page says "A qualified panel of judges will be announced soon!" and the rules say judges "may or may not be listed individually on the Hackathon Website, and may change before or during the Judging Period", so we cannot know whether an accessibility specialist, or anyone with clinical knowledge, will read our claims. We should write for a reader who has neither.
  5. Whether a priority category matters to a score is not stated. The six Fire TV priority categories are listed as priorities, and no criterion mentions them.
  6. Nothing on either page says how the project gallery, likes or any public vote affect judging. As far as both pages go, they do not.
  7. The Stage One reviewer's identity is only partly described. The rules say judging "may utilize expert panels, peer review, automated AI-driven analysis, or any combination thereof", so part of Stage One may be automated. A submission that only makes sense to a careful human reader is a risk.

9. How to use this file

docs/MOCK_JUDGE_1.md and docs/MOCK_JUDGE_2.md are two runs of this rubric against the repository by judges who did not write any of it. Each one runs the Stage One checklist in section 2.3, scores the four criteria in section 3 on the 1 to 5 scale in section 3.2, walks the friction log against the six fields in section 4, and ends with a ranked list of gaps.
